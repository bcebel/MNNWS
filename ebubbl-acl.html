ACL Weekend Photo Dump: How to Make a Shared Album That Actually Lasts

You're at ACL. You've taken 200 photos and videos this weekend. Your friends have taken 200 more. Everyone's posted a few to their story, where they'll disappear in 24 hours. By Monday, most of it is gone. The rest is scattered across six different phones and nobody's going to see any of it again.

That's the problem ebubbl (found at ebubbl.com) was built to solve. It's a shared album for a moment — a festival, a party, a night out, a trip — where everyone's photos and videos live together, on the phones of the people who were there. Not on a company's server. Not in someone's story that vanishes. Not dependent on Instagram existing next year.

What a Shared Album Actually Is

Instead of "social media" with the usual feed, followers, and algorithm, ebubbl uses Bubbles — private spaces defined by context. A bubble for ACL weekend. A bubble for your friend's birthday. A bubble for a specific bar's regulars. You invite the people who were there. They drop their photos in. That's it.

No stranger sees any of it. No algorithm decides which photos get shown to whom. No ad tech crawls it. The photos stay in the bubble, on the devices of the people in it.

Why It Works: The P2P Part

Under the hood, ebubbl uses WebTorrent — the same peer-to-peer technology that powers decentralized file sharing. When someone uploads a photo or video to a bubble, it gets distributed across everyone's device in that bubble. When someone else opens the album, they're pulling pieces from the people who were there, not from a central server.

A few things fall out of that:

  • Faster as more people join: the more people are in the bubble, the more sources there are for every photo and video.
  • No server costs for hosting everyone's media, which means no incentive to sell your data or cram in ads.
  • It keeps existing as long as the people who contributed still have it. Unlike Instagram, unlike a dead app, unlike a shutdown service.

For Bars, Venues, and Events

There's an obvious use case for a shared album that lives with the crowd instead of one company's cloud. A bar with a bubble for its regulars. A venue with a bubble for a specific night. A festival with a bubble for the weekend. The photos get contributed by the people who were there, and the whole thing stays alive as long as people care about it.

The Album That Doesn't Disappear

Social media has spent 15 years teaching us that content is disposable. You post it, an algorithm decides its fate, it fades, and eventually the platform does too. Bubbles are the opposite bet: content lives with the people who made it, and the network gets stronger as more people join.

If you're at ACL this weekend — or going to anything with a group of people who all took photos — make a bubble and dump them in. See how it feels to have a shared album that actually stays.
